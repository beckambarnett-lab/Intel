# EMBER — title art prompts

Title treatment is part of the image in every prompt below, not something pasted on after.

The feeling to chase, in order of importance:

1. **Something precious is running out.** Not danger — *loss*. The warmth in frame is finite and
   everyone looking at it knows.
2. **Smallness.** The dark isn't a threat so much as a scale. You are very little inside it.
3. **Tenderness.** The fire is the last kind thing in the world. That's what makes losing it hurt.
4. **Being watched, never seeing.** Implied entirely by how much of the frame is empty.

If a prompt starts explaining how light works, it's gone wrong. Describe what it feels like to be
the person standing there.

**Settled after the first round:** the two figures sit *touching*. The game exists to put friends in
a dark room together — the fire is the excuse, the company is the point, and every system underneath
agrees (you can't haul enough wood alone, a corpse drags at half weight with two, and revival
requires somebody else). Do not separate them for loneliness. The loneliness is the dark, not them.

Also settled: **a creature should be barely visible at the frame edge**, mostly obscured by a trunk,
at a size and contrast where a good share of viewers never find it. Ambiguity is the mechanic
(Q52) — an image that only some people realise is being watched is that mechanic as a poster. Do
not make it legible, and do not point at it.

## 1. Hero title art (primary)

> Title art for a survival horror game, overwhelmingly dark and quiet. Nearly the entire canvas is
> deep black, empty, patient. Low in the lower third, very small, a single fire burns in a clearing
> — a soft ragged bloom of warm orange no bigger than a coin in frame, the last warm thing in the
> world. Two tiny human figures sit close to it, shoulders touching, backs to us, rendered as barely
> more than smudges of shadow with orange edges. The forest around them is not drawn so much as
> withheld: a few pale trunk edges catching the light at the boundary, then nothing, an enormous
> soft void of black that fills the sky and the sides of the frame and feels like it is listening.
> Sparks drift up and die out. Set across the upper middle of the black, the single word "EMBER" in
> a tall, wide-tracked, condensed serif — the letters are unlit and near-black, carved and cracked
> like charred wood, catching only the faintest orange from the fire far below them, with hairline
> fractures inside the strokes glowing dimly like coals about to go out. The type feels ancient and
> cooling. Restrained, aching, lonely, elegiac. Colour is almost absent: firelight orange and coal
> red are the only saturated notes, everything else near-black and cold ash grey. Heavy film grain,
> crushed blacks, no moonlight, no blue, no fill light, no glow effects outside the letterforms.
> Painterly, charcoal and ink, more suggestion than detail. --ar 16:9

### v2 refinement pass

The first generation landed. Two corrections carried forward — the letterform cracks came out as
bright molten veins (reads as fantasy; the word is *ember*, the coals should be nearly out), and the
glowing ground debris spread the light source into a scatter instead of one small fire.

> Same composition. The cracks in the letterforms are far dimmer and sparser — dull dark red, nearly
> extinguished, mostly cold grey charred stone with only a few faint coals surviving near the bases
> of the letters, no bright molten veins, no orange saturation in the type. Remove the scattered
> glowing debris on the ground; the fire is the single point source and the ground around it falls
> off quickly into black.

Worth keeping from that generation: the trunks resolved into a symmetrical corridor, which reads as
a nave — the fire lands at the altar and the two figures become supplicants. Ask for it explicitly
next time: *"receding trunks form a symmetrical corridor, cathedral-like, fire at the vanishing
point."*

## 2. Intimate variant — the hands

> Close, tender, and almost entirely dark. A pair of bare hands cupped around a small heap of dying
> embers, seen from just above — the only light in the image, low and red-orange, catching the
> knuckles, the underside of a jaw, a breath of ash in the air. Everything past the wrists falls
> instantly into black. The gesture reads as protective and futile at once, like sheltering a candle
> outdoors. Behind and above, floating in the dark, the word "EMBER" in tall widely-spaced charred
> letterforms, dim, almost unlit, faintly warmed from below by the embers in the hands. Enormous
> negative space. The feeling is quiet grief and stubborn care, not fear. Near-monochrome, only the
> coals are saturated. Film grain, deep blacks, no other light source. --ar 16:9

## 3. Scale variant — the walk out

> One small figure with their back to us, walking away into a forest at night, carrying a lantern
> that lights maybe a metre around their boots and nothing else. They are tiny — occupying a
> fraction of the lower frame — and the darkness above and around them is vast, soft and total,
> more weather than space. Far behind them at the very edge of the frame, a faint warm smudge on the
> horizon: the fire they left, already almost too distant to see. The word "EMBER" sits large across
> the upper dark, near-black charred serif letterforms with the faintest ember warmth in their
> cracks, so quiet you almost miss it. It should feel like homesickness. Lonely, resigned, cold.
> Firelight is the only colour; everything else is ash grey and black. Heavy grain, crushed blacks,
> no moon, no stars, no blue. --ar 21:9

## Negative prompt

`bright, high key, blue moonlight, teal or purple darkness, ambient fill light, lens flare, god
rays, glowing eyes, visible monster, gore, action pose, weapons drawn, cozy campfire marshmallow
vibe, fantasy, magic effects, neon, oversaturated, HDR, clean digital gloss, busy composition,
detailed background, HUD, watermark, generic horror font, metallic chrome logo, outer glow on text`

## Notes

- **The fire should be small.** Every draft will want to make it big and heroic. Push it back:
  *"smaller, further, almost lost in the frame."* The moment it dominates, the dread evaporates.
- **Don't let the type light up.** A glowing logo reads as fantasy. Ask for the letters to be
  *"unlit, cooling, warmed only from outside, glow confined to fine cracks."*
- **Emptiness is the subject.** If a generator fills the black with trees and detail, say
  *"90% of the canvas is empty black, no background detail, withhold everything."*
- If the mood comes out scary rather than sad, add *"melancholy, elegiac, no threat visible"* —
  fear is what the game does to you later; the title art's job is to make you care about the fire
  first.
- Ratios: `16:9` key art · `21:9` banner/capsule · `2:3` poster · `1:1` icon (use the hands).
